About Me

Projects

Do Joint Language-Audio Embeddings Encode Perceptual Timbre Semantics?

ISMIR 2026

This work investigates whether joint language-audio embedding models, including MS-CLAP, LAION-CLAP, and MuQ-MuLan, encode perceptually meaningful dimensions of timbre, such as brightness, roughness, and warmth. By comparing model similarities with human perceptual judgments across instrument sounds and audio effects, we find that LAION-CLAP demonstrates the most consistent alignment with human timbre perception.

HARP 3.0: Generalizing I/O and API Support for Machine Learning in Digital Audio Workstations

NeurIPS AI4Music Workshop 2025

HARP is a sample editor that enables asynchronous audio and multimodal processing with remotely hosted machine learning models. It can operate as a standalone application or integrate into a DAW workflow, routing audio, MIDI, text, and metadata to models hosted locally or remotely through web APIs.

HARP is a collaborative project between the Interactive Audio Lab at Northwestern University and the Audio Information Research Group at the University of Rochester. My contributions include expanding model and API support, improving generalized input/output workflows, and developing additional features for the HARP ecosystem.

ComposerX: Multi-Agent Symbolic Music Composition with LLMs

ISMIR 2024

ComposerX is a multi-agent symbolic music composition framework powered by large language models. It organizes multiple LLM agents into specialized roles, including composition, arrangement, review, and coordination, allowing them to collaboratively generate and refine symbolic music from natural-language instructions.

Our results show that the multi-agent framework improves musical coherence and melody quality compared with single-agent baselines, demonstrating the potential of collaborative LLM systems for controllable music composition.
